The Ethical Hooper Index

Who scores through skill, and who scores through the whistle? Thirty NBA seasons, measured.

Every pickup run has one: the player who never seems to need a whistle. No arm-hooks, no launching sideways into a closeout, no head-snap on contact. Just footwork, a jumper, and a bucket. Basketball has a name for that player: the ethical hooper. This project turns the slang into a number.

What is an ethical hooper?

An ethical hooper is a player who scores and dominates through pure skill, clean footwork, natural shot-making, and fluid movement, rather than trickery, flopping, or foul-baiting.

  • No rule manipulation. They don’t jump into defenders, hook arms, or sell contact with exaggerated head-snaps to earn a whistle.
  • An aesthetic playstyle. Their offense runs on clean mid-range pull-ups, pure jump shots, smooth handles, and honest post moves.
  • Low free-throw reliance. They take few free throws relative to their scoring volume, beyond what their shot diet would naturally earn.

In measurable terms: an ethical hooper generates scoring through skill rather than the whistle: high shot-making relative to shot difficulty, self-created offense, clean footwork, and low dependence on free throws that are not explained by an aggressive, rim-attacking shot diet. A center who lives at the rim should shoot free throws. A guard who draws three-shot fouls by leaning into closeouts is a different story.

This is a stylistic metric, not a moral judgment of any person. It uses on-court play-by-play data only. Drawing fouls is legal and often smart basketball. The index describes how a player scores, nothing about who they are.

Look up any player: the Player Search page charts a player’s Ethical Hooper Index season by season.

The map

The index has two axes. Skill Score (x) blends shot-making above expectation, self-creation, shot difficulty, and clean footwork. Whistle Score (y, flipped so “clean” is up) blends excess free throws, three-point fouls drawn, free-throw share of points, and perimeter and-ones. Every value is measured against the same season’s peers, so a 1999 post-up big and a 2025 pull-up guard are compared on equal footing.

The Ethical Hooper Index (EHI) combines both axes on a 0–100 scale where 50 is an average qualifying scorer. Because the slang is mostly about not living at the line, the whistle axis carries 60% of the weight and skill 40%. An ethical hooper doesn’t have to be the most skilled player on the floor, just a clean one.

The four archetypes

Each player lands in one corner of the map, depending on whether they are above or below their season’s average on each axis.

Archetype What it means Careers Examples (most minutes played)
Ethical Hooper Skilled scorer who rarely needs the whistle: makes tough shots, creates their own offense, and gets few free throws for their shot mix. 117 LeBron James, Kevin Garnett, Ray Allen
Whistle Artist Just as skilled, but works the whistle: draws fouls on jump shots and threes, and leans on the free-throw line. 96 Dirk Nowitzki, Kobe Bryant, Tim Duncan
Downhill Bully Less efficient scorer who lives at the line through contact on drives and in the paint, not on jumpers. 92 Paul Pierce, Russell Westbrook, Stephon Marbury
Grinder Less efficient, but clean: takes tough or low-percentage shots without seeking contact, and rarely gets to the line. 112 Carmelo Anthony, Vince Carter, Shawn Marion

Labels are relative to each season’s peers, so a player close to average on an axis can switch types from year to year.

Top-line findings

  • Steve Nash is the most ethical hooper of the last 30 years (career EHI 87.7 over 8 qualifying seasons), combining elite shot-making (+2.76 SD) with a light whistle (-1.17 SD). James Harden ranks last of 417 qualifying careers (EHI 10.7, Whistle Score +3.60 SD).
  • The style is a real, persistent trait. A player’s EHI correlates r = 0.74 with their EHI the next season (2,708 season pairs).
  • The NBA’s 2021-22 crackdown on “non-basketball moves” left a visible mark. League-wide, three-point shooting fouls fell from 1.42 to 1.08 per 100 attempts in one season. Players our index had flagged as whistle-dependent lost efficiency afterward: each standard deviation of pre-rule Whistle Score predicted a 0.38-point drop in true shooting (p = 0.049), while a placebo “rule” in 2019-20 shows nothing (p = 0.814).
  • Ethical hoopers are common. 117 of 417 careers land in the Ethical Hooper quadrant, including Payton Pritchard, Nikola Vučević and Chris Paul.
  • In 2025-26, the cleanest qualifying scorers were Bobby Portis, Payton Pritchard and Jrue Holiday.
