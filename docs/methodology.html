Methodology

From six million shots to one number

Data

Source What it provides Seasons
NBA.com play-by-play (PlayByPlayV3), via the shufinskiy/nba_data bulk archive Every shot, foul, free throw, turnover and assist 1996-97 → 2025-26, regular season + playoffs
NBA.com shot charts (same archive) Shot x/y location, zone, distance, shot type 1996-97 → 2025-26
Basketball Reference Official minutes, games, position; independent cross-check tables 1996-97 → 2025-26
pbpstats.com Offensive possessions; second cross-check 2000-01 → 2025-26

Why 1996-97? It is the first season with NBA.com play-by-play and shot-location data, both required for every component. Starting there keeps all thirty seasons on one measurement system.

Data quality. The play-by-play matches official totals: for all player-seasons with 1,000+ minutes, field-goal attempts, free-throw attempts and turnovers agree with Basketball Reference within ±2 in every case. On a random sample of 25 player-seasons, the counts that drive this index agree closely with Basketball Reference’s independent play-by-play tables (correlations 0.998–1.000). Details on the Validation page.

Tracking data (Tier 2). The original design included NBA player-tracking data (drives, defender distance, 2013-14+) as a validation layer. The NBA’s stats server blocks automated requests, so that layer isn’t included yet. It was never an input to the index itself.

Cleaning: how each event is identified

  • Free throws drawn are credited to the free-throw shooter. Play-by-play logs the fouler on the foul row, not the fouled player.
  • Three-shot fouls: “free throw 1 of 3” trips. The fouled missed three is not logged as an attempt, so the denominator is three-point attempts plus three-shot trips.
  • And-ones: a “1 of 1” free throw by a player who made a field goal at the same game-clock moment (98% of all 1-of-1 trips; most of the rest are away-from-play and, since 2022-23, transition take fouls).
  • Self-creation: a made field goal with no assist credited.
  • Heaves (36+ feet with ≤ 3 seconds left in a period) are excluded everywhere. From 2025-26 the NBA logs missed heaves as team attempts, so excluding them keeps all seasons on one definition.
  • The 2012-13 season was missing its final 16 games in the primary archive. They were restored from the older-format feed using an event-code mapping learned from the 1,213 games present in both.

The expected-FG (xFG) model

To reward making hard shots, we first need to know how hard each shot was. The xFG model estimates the probability that a shot goes in from where and how it was taken: location, distance, angle, zone, shot family (jumper, layup, dunk, hook, tip), whether it came off a drive or on the move, period, game clock and season. It never sees who took the shot, otherwise it would absorb the very skill we are trying to measure.

Shot-type labels get more detailed over time (9 types in 1996-97, 50 by 2007-08; “step-back” and “pull-up” don’t exist early on), so the model uses five shot families that exist in every season.

The model is gradient-boosted trees (xgboost) trained on 5,841,268 shots from 1996-97 to 2023-24 (6,307,970 in total), tuned on 2024-25, and evaluated on the held-out 2025-26 season:

xFG improves log-loss by 2.7% over a zone-average baseline and is closely calibrated: shots predicted to go in 60% of the time go in about 60% of the time.

Distance (46% of the model’s gain) and shot family (32%) do most of the work. For the final index, each shot’s xFG is cross-fitted: games are split into five folds, and every shot is scored by a model that never saw its game.

Excess free-throw rate is the key whistle metric. Rim-attackers should shoot more free throws, so a regression predicts each player’s shooting-foul free-throw rate from shot diet (rim, paint, mid-range, corner-three and three-point shares), a usage proxy, and position, fitted separately for each era with season fixed effects. The residual, free throws beyond what that profile earns, is the excess. This separates a center finishing through contact from a guard manufacturing fouls on jumpers.

Only shooting-foul free throws count. Intentional fouls (hack-a-big, fouling a good free-throw shooter late in a close game) are the defense’s strategy, not the shooter’s foul-seeking, and would otherwise penalize elite free-throw shooters for being fouled on purpose.

Shrinkage, standardization, and the composite

  1. Empirical-Bayes shrinkage. A 1,000-minute season still contains only a few dozen three-shot fouls or and-ones, so raw rates for small samples produce fake extremes. Each count rate is shrunk toward its season mean with a beta-binomial prior; continuous measures (shot-making, difficulty, excess FT rate) use a normal-normal prior. Priors are estimated each season from players with reliable samples.
  2. Within-season z-scores. Every component is standardized against the same season’s qualifying scorers, which neutralizes era-wide shifts (the 2004-05 hand-check removal, the three-point boom, league-wide whistle trends). Each component’s z is capped at ±4 so no single metric can dominate.
  3. Two axes. Skill Score and Whistle Score are the weighted means of their components’ z-scores, each re-standardized within season.
  4. EHI = 0.4 × Skill − 0.6 × Whistle, re-standardized within season and mapped to 0–100 (50 = average qualifier, 15 points = one standard deviation).
  5. Archetypes come from the signs of the two axes: Ethical Hooper (skill ≥ 0, whistle < 0), Whistle Artist (skill ≥ 0, whistle ≥ 0), Downhill Bully (skill < 0, whistle ≥ 0), Grinder (skill < 0, whistle < 0).

Why these weights

  • Whistle outweighs skill (60% / 40%). In the slang, an ethical hooper doesn’t have to be the most skilled player, but they cannot live at the line.
  • Shot-making above expected gets the largest skill weight because it is the most direct measure of skill net of shot selection. Self-creation captures handles and shot creation. Difficulty and footwork are supporting signals.
  • Excess FT rate gets the largest whistle weight because it is the cleanest whistle measure net of shot diet. Three-point fouls are the purest jump-shot foul-drawing signal available back to 1996-97. FT share of points captures raw free-throw reliance relative to scoring volume. Perimeter and-ones are noisier, so they get the least weight.

Who qualifies

A hooper is a real scorer: a regular-season player-season qualifies with ≥ 1,000 minutes (prorated in the shortened 1998-99, 2011-12, 2019-20 and 2020-21 seasons) and ≥ 12 field-goal attempts per 36 minutes. Role players who rarely shoot shouldn’t top the “ethical” list by default. Playoffs use ≥ 150 minutes. Careers need ≥ 4 qualifying seasons. Career EHI is the minutes-weighted average; peak EHI is the best minutes-weighted three-consecutive-qualifying-season stretch.

That yields 4,022 regular-season and 1,302 playoff player-seasons, and 417 careers.

Changes from the original design

Each was triggered by a result that looked wrong, checked against the data, and documented in the project’s NOTES.md:

  • “Share of and-ones on jumpers” became perimeter and-ones per 100 perimeter attempts. The share version mostly measured shot diet (it tracked a player’s share of jump shots closely), so a pure spot-up shooter whose handful of and-ones all came on jumpers looked like a foul-baiter.
  • Free-throw metrics use shooting-foul free throws only, removing intentional fouls (see above).
  • Usage is approximated by scoring attempts per 36 minutes, since true usage rate needs team possession data from the blocked stats server. Within-season standardization makes the two nearly equivalent.
  • Whistle weighted 60/40 over skill, reflecting the slang definition.

Limitations

  • No defender distance before 2013-14. xFG knows where and how a shot was taken, not how well it was contested. A player who takes many tightly-contested shots will look like a better shot-maker than one who takes open looks from the same spots.
  • Missed-shot fouls can only be located for threes. Play-by-play records a shooting foul on a missed two but not where the shot was, so jump-shot foul-baiting on twos is only visible through and-ones and the excess-FT residual.
  • Officiating varies by crew, season and arena. Within-season standardization removes league-wide shifts but not crew-to-crew noise.
  • Flopping is only recorded in play-by-play from 2023-24 and is too sparse to use.
  • Style, not morality. Drawing fouls is legal and frequently smart. A low EHI means a player scored differently, not wrongly.
